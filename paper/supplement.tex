\section{Scope and frozen outputs}
The confirmatory benchmark comprises 101 public trait--dataset tasks from 12
panels, seven neural architectures, four losses, ten cross-validation test folds
and three calibration maps, plus GBLUP and ridge baselines. The assembled file
\texttt{results/results\_main\_campaign.parquet} contains 90,900 unique rows.
The nested sensitivity file
\texttt{results/results\_loss\_specific\_wheat.parquet} contains 1,080 rows.
No private dataset is used. The analysis validates row counts, experimental keys
and task coverage before writing \texttt{results/analysis\_final/}.

The main campaign has 588 missing Pearson/Spearman values, 270 missing $R^2$ and
NDCG@$10$ values, and 334 missing calibration slopes/intercepts. Missing
correlations are concentrated in nearly constant folds of the rice seed-color
trait. RMSE is complete. Aggregation uses available finite folds and reports the
resulting task count.

\section{Estimands and inference}
Raw Pearson is primary, raw NDCG@$10$ secondary, and affine NRMSE is the principal
scale outcome. NRMSE divides RMSE by the phenotype SD in the corresponding test
fold. Folds are averaged first. For a pooled effect, architectures are averaged
within task, then the 12 panel means receive equal weight. Intervals are based on
20,000 panel-cluster bootstrap samples. Exact sign-flip tests reverse every task
within a panel together. Trait-weighted and leave-one-panel-out estimates are
available in the machine-readable table.

\section{Corroborative interaction models}
The mixed model uses panel as a fixed blocking factor and a random intercept for
trait--dataset task. Full models contain loss-by-architecture interaction terms;
likelihood-ratio tests compare them with additive models. The interaction is not
confirmed at 5\% for any of the three endpoints, so architecture-specific
intervals are interpreted descriptively.

\section{Calibration}
The theoretical rank-preservation result requires a positive affine slope. The
benchmark used unconstrained least squares fitted on validation predictions. Of
7,014 Pearson-trained fold cells with finite raw and affine correlation, 12
(0.17\%) changed sign. Raw predictions are therefore used for all correlation
and upper-tail claims.

\section{Loss-specific tuning and initialization}
For each of MLP, CNN and Transformer and each of five outer folds, MSE and
Pearson used identical banks of eight candidates and two inner folds. Selection
used Pearson on environment-1 outer-training individuals; the winner was then
evaluated on four wheat environments with seeds 0, 1 and 2. Thus the four
outcomes are transfers from one tuning phenotype, not four independently tuned
experiments. Only selected configurations and the nested trial ledger are
retained; the main campaign retains selected configurations but not its complete
trial ledger.

\section{Absolute method ranks}
Ranks compare all 30 methods within each task using raw predictions and then
average the 101 tasks with equal trait weight (unlike the panel-balanced primary
estimand). Ridge and
GBLUP rank first and second on average; neural-loss comparisons should therefore
be understood as within-architecture improvements rather than superiority over
linear genomic prediction.

\section{Figure source data and reproducibility}
Every figure is exported as PDF, SVG and PNG. Machine-readable panel data are in
\texttt{figures/source\_data/}. The primary analysis is regenerated with
\texttt{python analysis/final\_analysis.py}, interaction models with
\texttt{python analysis/mixed\_model\_final.py}, tables with
\texttt{python analysis/make\_final\_supplement.py}, and figures with
\texttt{python figures/make\_figures.py}. The main benchmark uses one neural
initialization per fold; only the wheat sensitivity quantifies three-seed
variation.
